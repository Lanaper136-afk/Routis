1. The basics: who is responsibleRules H1–H3 and the Introduction
  • MUST / MUST NOT means a legal requirement. Breaking it is a criminal offence and can lead to fines, penalty points, disqualification or, in the most serious cases, prison.
  • Should / should not (and do / do not) is advice. It cannot by itself lead to prosecution, but the Code can be used as evidence in court to show who was at fault.
  • Rule H1: the people in charge of the most dangerous vehicles bear the greatest responsibility to take care and reduce the danger they pose to others.
  • Rule H2: at a junction, give way to pedestrians crossing or waiting to cross the road you are turning into or out of. You MUST give way to pedestrians on a zebra crossing.
  • Rule H3: do not cut across cyclists, horse riders or horse-drawn vehicles going ahead when you turn or change lane. Wait for a safe gap in the flow of cyclists if necessary.

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules H1–H3 and the Introduction).

2. Speed limitsRules 124–125
  • The speed limit is the absolute maximum. It does not mean it is safe to drive at that speed whatever the conditions (Rule 125).
  • Driving too fast for the road and traffic conditions is dangerous. Slow down for hazardous bends, vulnerable road users, bad weather and at night.
  • In Wales the built-up limit is 20 mph. In England and Scotland it is 30 mph.

3. Stopping distances and gapsRule 126
  • Drive at a speed that lets you stop well within the distance you can see to be clear. Never get closer than the overall stopping distance.
  • On high-speed roads leave at least a two-second gap from the vehicle in front.
  • In the wet, at least double the gap. On icy roads it can be up to ten times greater.
  • Large vehicles and motorcycles need more distance to stop. In a tunnel, large vehicles should leave a four-second gap, and if you have to stop leave at least 5 metres.

4. Lanes and overtakingRules 133–135 and 162–169
  • Before changing lanes: mirrors, a quick sideways glance if needed, signal, then move only when it is safe (Rule 133).
  • In congestion do not change lanes unnecessarily (Rule 134).
  • On a three-lane single carriageway use the middle lane only for overtaking or turning right, and do not use the right-hand lane (Rule 135).
  • Overtake only when it is safe and legal. When overtaking cyclists at up to 30 mph, leave at least 1.5 metres (Rule 163).
  • You MUST NOT overtake across double white lines with a solid line nearest to you, or after a No Overtaking sign (Rule 165).
  • Do not overtake if there is any doubt: approaching a corner, a bend, a hump bridge or the brow of a hill (Rule 166). Do not overtake at junctions or where the road narrows (Rule 167).
  • Never obstruct drivers who wish to pass (Rule 168).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 133–135 and 162–169).

5. Junctions and turningRules 170–183
  • Take extra care at junctions. Do not cross or join a road until there is a gap large enough to do so safely (Rule 170).
  • At broken white lines across the road you MUST give way to traffic on the main road (Rule 172).
  • Box junctions (yellow criss-cross): you MUST NOT enter the box until your exit road or lane is clear (Rule 174).
  • Turning right: do not cut the corner. The usual safer method at crossroads is to turn right side to right side (Rules 179–181).
  • Turning left: do not overtake just before you turn left, and keep as close to the left as is safe and practicable (Rules 182–183).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 170–183).

6. RoundaboutsRules 184–190
  • Give priority to traffic approaching from your right, unless signs, road markings or traffic lights say otherwise (Rule 185).
  • To take the first exit on the left, signal left and approach in the left-hand lane. For an exit to the right or a full circle, signal right and approach in the right-hand lane (Rule 186).
  • Mini-roundabouts: all vehicles MUST pass round the central markings, except large vehicles that physically cannot. Avoid U-turns at mini-roundabouts (Rule 188).
  • At double mini-roundabouts treat each one separately and give way to traffic from the right (Rules 189–190).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 184–190).

7. Traffic lightsLight signals controlling traffic; Rules 175–176
  • Red: stop and wait behind the stop line. You MUST NOT go over the line while the light is red.
  • Red and amber: still stop. Do not pass or start until green shows.
  • Green: you may go on if the way is clear. Take extra care when turning, and give way to pedestrians who are crossing.
  • Amber: stop at the stop line. You may go on only if the amber appears after you have crossed the line, or you are so close that stopping would be unsafe.
  • Green arrow: if the way is clear you may go, but only in the direction of the arrow.
  • Alternately flashing red lights mean you must stop. They are used at level crossings, lifting bridges, airfields, fire stations and similar places.

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Light signals controlling traffic; Rules 175–176).

8. Pedestrian crossingsRules 191–199
  • You MUST NOT park on a crossing or in the area covered by the zig-zag lines (Rule 191).
  • Zebra crossing: you MUST give way once a pedestrian has moved onto the crossing (Rule 195). Parallel crossings add a cycle route alongside the stripes.
  • Pelican crossing: when the amber light is flashing, you MUST give way to any pedestrians on the crossing (Rule 196).
  • Puffin, toucan and equestrian crossings have no flashing amber phase. If the crossing is not working, proceed with extreme caution (Rule 199).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 191–199).

9. Alcohol, drugs and tirednessRules 91, 95, 96
  • England and Wales: the limit is 80 mg of alcohol per 100 ml of blood (35 µg per 100 ml of breath).
  • Scotland: the limit is lower: 50 mg per 100 ml of blood (22 µg per 100 ml of breath).
  • But any amount of alcohol affects your ability to drive safely, and alcohol can still be in your body the next morning (Rule 95).
  • You MUST NOT drive under the influence of drugs or medicine, or with certain drugs above the legal limit. Ask a doctor or pharmacist about medicines (Rule 96).
  • Tiredness: do not start a journey when tired and avoid long journeys between midnight and 6 am. Take a break of at least 15 minutes after every two hours of driving. If sleepy, stop in a safe place (not the hard shoulder) (Rule 91).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 91, 95, 96).

10. Seat belts, child seats and phonesRules 99–102 and 149–150
  • Drivers MUST wear a seat belt if one is fitted. The driver is responsible for children under 14 wearing a belt or using an approved child restraint (Rules 99–100).
  • Children under 3 must use the correct child restraint. From 3 until they reach 1.35 m or their 12th birthday (whichever comes first) they must use the correct child restraint. Above that height, or aged 12–13, a seat belt is required if available.
  • A rear-facing baby seat MUST NOT be fitted in a seat protected by an active frontal airbag (Rule 101).
  • Let children out through the kerb-side door and use child locks where fitted (Rule 102).
  • You MUST NOT use a hand-held mobile phone while driving or supervising a learner. The ban applies even if the device’s communication functions are off (Rule 149). Hands-free use is also likely to distract you.
  • Do not rely on driver-assistance systems: you stay responsible for the vehicle (Rule 150).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 99–102 and 149–150).

11. Waiting and parkingRules 238–252
  • Double yellow lines: no waiting at any time. Single yellow lines: no waiting during the times on nearby plates (Rule 238).
  • Red routes: a double red line always means no stopping at any time, and a single red line means no stopping during the times shown on signs (see Road markings in the Code). Rule 240 also bans stopping or parking on pedestrian crossings and clearways.
  • Do not stop or park near a school entrance, at a bus or tram stop, or on a bend. Do not park opposite or within 10 metres (32 feet) of a junction unless in an authorised space (Rule 243).
  • You MUST NOT park in spaces reserved for others, such as Blue Badge holders (Rule 241).
  • Pavement parking is not allowed in London or Scotland. Elsewhere, only where signs permit (Rule 244).
  • At night, do not park facing against the traffic flow outside a recognised space (Rule 248). Vehicles on roads with a limit above 30 mph must show parking lights (Rule 249).
  • On a hill, park close to the kerb with the handbrake on. Facing uphill, turn the wheels away from the kerb. Facing downhill, turn them towards the kerb (Rule 252).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 238–252).

12. MotorwaysRules 253–274
  • Learners with a provisional car licence may use a motorway only with an approved instructor in a car with L plates (D plates in Wales) and dual controls (Rules 253–254).
  • Joining: use the left slip road, give priority to traffic already on the motorway, and match its speed in the left lane (Rule 259).
  • Keep to the left lane unless overtaking. Overtake only on the right. Never overtake on the left or weave between lanes (Rules 264, 267–268).
  • Never reverse, cross the central reservation or drive against the traffic. If you miss your exit, carry on to the next one (Rule 263).
  • Red X or red flashing lights: the lane is closed. Never drive in a closed lane (Rule 258). Flashing amber lights warn of a hazard ahead: reduce speed (Rule 257).
  • Use the hard shoulder only in an emergency or when told to by police, traffic officers or a sign (Rule 269). Do not stop on the carriageway, slip roads or verges except in an emergency (Rule 271).
  • Do not stop to make or take phone calls unless it is an emergency (Rule 271).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 253–274).

13. WeatherRules 226–237
  • Use headlights when visibility is seriously reduced, generally when you cannot see more than 100 metres. Use fog lights only in those conditions and switch them off when visibility improves (Rules 226, 236).
  • Wet: stopping distances are at least double. Keep well back. If the steering feels unresponsive, ease off the accelerator and slow down gradually (Rule 227).
  • Snow and ice: avoid driving unless the journey is essential. Clear snow and ice from all windows and lights before setting off. Stopping distances can be ten times greater. Drive slowly in as high a gear as possible, and accelerate and brake gently (Rules 228–231).
  • Wind: gusts can push cars, cyclists and motorcyclists off course, especially on bridges and open roads (Rule 232).
  • Fog: check your mirrors and slow down before entering it. Be able to pull up well within the distance you can see clearly. Do not speed up to escape a close vehicle behind (Rules 234–235).
  • Hot weather: keep the vehicle ventilated to avoid drowsiness. Roads can soften or become slippery (Rule 237).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 226–237).

14. Vulnerable road usersRules 204–215
  • Children may step into the road unexpectedly. Drive slowly near schools and in residential streets (Rules 205–208).
  • Older pedestrians may need more time to cross: be patient. At 20 mph there is only a 1 in 20 chance of a pedestrian being killed (Rule 207).
  • Cyclists and motorcyclists are hard to see: look carefully before you emerge, as they may be approaching faster than you think (Rule 211).
  • Give cyclists and motorcyclists at least as much room as you would give a car when overtaking, and more in bad weather and at night (Rule 212).
  • Cyclists may ride in the centre of the lane on narrow roads, and in groups two abreast (Rule 213).
  • Horses: slow to a maximum of 10 mph, pass wide and slowly allowing at least 2 metres, and do not sound your horn or rev your engine (Rule 215).
  • Large vehicles need extra road space to turn, and their drivers may not see you in the mirrors. Many are limited to 56 mph (Rules 221–222).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 204–215).

15. Breakdowns and crashesRules 275–287
  • Get your vehicle off the road if possible. Use hazard warning lights if it causes an obstruction. If you have a warning triangle, place it at least 45 metres behind the vehicle, but never use one on a motorway (Rule 276).
  • On a motorway, move left and switch on your hazard lights. If you cannot get out safely, stay in the vehicle with your seat belt on and call 999 or use the SOS button (Rule 277).
  • At a crash scene: stop in a safe place with hazard lights on, ask drivers to turn off engines and stop smoking, and call 999 with the exact location (Rule 283).
  • Do not move injured people unless they are in immediate danger. Do not remove a motorcyclist’s helmet unless it is essential (Rule 283).
  • If a collision causes damage or injury you MUST stop and give your name, address and vehicle details to anyone with a reasonable need for them. If you did not, report to the police as soon as possible and within 24 hours (Rule 286).

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Rules 275–287).

16. Penalty points and new driversAnnex 5
  • Hand-held mobile phone use while driving: 6 penalty points.
  • Speeding: 3 to 6 points (3 with a fixed penalty). Traffic light offence: 3 points. Driving without insurance: 6 to 8 points.
  • Driving unfit through drink or drugs, or with excess alcohol: disqualification is obligatory (minimum 12 months).
  • 12 or more points within 3 years means disqualification for at least 6 months.
  • New drivers: if you get 6 or more points within 2 years of passing your first test (including points from before the test), your licence is revoked. You must reapply for a provisional licence and drive as a learner until you pass the test again.

Source: The Highway Code on GOV.UK (Annex 5).
