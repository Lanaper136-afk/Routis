UK road markings and traffic lights

Markings on the road have the same force as signs. Traffic lights give you a clear order.

14 road markings

Double white lines (solid nearest you)

Two solid white lines along the middle of the road. You must not cross or straddle them if the line nearest you is solid.

Overtaking is not allowed here, except to pass a stationary vehicle or a very slow one.

Centre line

A broken white line with short markings and long gaps along the middle of the road.

You may cross it to overtake when it is safe and legal.

Hazard warning line

A broken white line with long markings and short gaps.

A hazard is ahead. Do not cross it unless the road is clearly safe.

Single yellow line

A single yellow line along the edge of the road.

Waiting is restricted at the times shown on nearby plates.

Double yellow lines

Two yellow lines along the edge of the road.

No waiting at any time, unless a sign says otherwise.

Red route line

A red line along the edge of the road on a red route.

Do not stop except in marked bays and for the times allowed.

Zigzag lines at a crossing

White zigzag lines on each side of a pedestrian crossing.

No parking, and no overtaking the moving vehicle nearest the crossing.

Yellow box junction

Yellow criss-cross lines painted on a junction.

Do not enter unless your exit is clear, except when turning right and held up by oncoming traffic.

Stop line

A solid white line across your lane at a Stop sign.

You must stop behind it and give way before you move on.

Give way line

Two broken white lines across your lane.

Give way to traffic on the main road before you go on.

Bus lane markings

A lane marked BUS LANE with a solid or broken white line.

Do not drive in it during the hours shown on the sign.

Cycle lane (solid white line)

A cycle lane marked off by a solid white line.

You must not drive or park in it while it is in operation.

School Keep Clear

Yellow zigzag lines outside a school entrance.

Do not stop or park there, even to set down or pick up children.

Zebra crossing markings

White stripes across the road with flashing amber beacons nearby.

Give way to anyone waiting to cross or already on the crossing.

Traffic lights

Red traffic light

A steady red light.

Stop behind the line and wait.

Red and amber traffic lights

Red and amber shown together.

Stop and wait. Do not go until the green light shows.

Green traffic light

A steady green light.

Go only if the way is clear and safe.

Amber traffic light

A steady amber light.

Stop at the line, unless you have already crossed it or are so close that stopping might cause an accident.
